# Learning guidelines — the SLA contract for this course (pt-BR → Japanese, zero→N5→N4)

> Working contract distilled from verified research (Phase R deep-research, 2026-06). Internal prose is English;
> every learner-facing EXAMPLE is natural pt-BR. Each rule is one line + a parenthetical *why*. This file governs
> authoring and the lesson-auditor; it complements (does not replace) `quality_rubric.md` and `curriculum.md`.
> Callouts in lessons use the BLOCK TYPE only (`<note type="l1-advantage|l1-pitfall|tip|warning|culture|example">`,
> labels "Vantagem PT" / "Armadilha PT") — never an emoji.

## A. The SLA engine

### Retrieval (testing effect)
- DO put ≥1 active-recall and ≥1 production exercise in every content lesson (retrieval beats restudy, g≈+0.51; vs filler g≈+0.93 — Adesope 2017).
- DO design the FIRST retrieval of any item to succeed (~70%+): only successful first retrieval yields long-term gains; failed retrieval ≈ restudy (Karpicke).
- DO give immediate corrective feedback on every recall attempt (feedback is what rescues low-success retrievals — Karpicke; spec §1.2).
- DON'T rely on rereading / re-recognition to teach (passive rereading creates an illusion of learning and overconfidence — Roediger).
- DON'T read "MC > short-answer on average" as "prefer multiple-choice" (the MC edge is format-match + feedback dependent; production transfers better — Adesope 2017). Keep the mix.

### Spacing
- DO schedule each item on an expanding lag across lessons (introduce → ~D1 → ~D3 → ~D7 → …): spaced beats massed (Kim & Webb 2022).
- DO size the first gap as a PROPORTION of the target horizon that SHRINKS as the horizon grows (~20–40% for a 1-week goal, ~5–10% for a 1-year goal — Cepeda 2008).
- DO ensure massed first exposure inside a lesson is followed by spaced review later (massed gives high initial success but poor retention — Cepeda).
- DON'T bunch all reviews of an item into one lesson and never revisit it (no spacing = no durable retention).

### Coverage (vocabulary selection)
- DO author each controlled text so known-token coverage is ~100% within the lesson's cumulative introduced set (i+1; the 95/98% literature is for UNASSISTED authentic reading, not beginner texts — Laufer & Ravenhorst-Kalovski).
- DO use the 95–98% / 4–5k–8k-family findings only to justify frequency-ordered SELECTION, never as a per-lesson coverage target (category error if applied to a zero→N4 course).
- DON'T introduce a token in a sentence that the learner has not met, except the lesson's single i+1 item (uncontrolled coverage tanks comprehension).

### Output (pushed production)
- DO include ≥1 sentence-construction / speaking task with an answer key per content lesson (comprehensible input is necessary but NOT sufficient; immersion leaves persistent production errors — Swain).
- DO use task repetition (say it, then say it again) for fluency (immediate same-task repetition raised speech rate at every proficiency level and task type; gains largest across the first three PERFORMANCES and still rising through the fifth, of six — [Lambert, Kormos & Minn 2017, SSLA 39(1) 167–196](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263116000085)). Note the unit: six performances = one original plus five repetitions, so "three performances" is the first attempt plus two.

### Interleaving
- DO introduce a set BLOCKED, then interleave only CONFUSABLE/high-similarity items afterward (は/が, に/で, transitive/intransitive, look-alike kana/kanji, length minimal pairs) — interleaving's benefit rises with between-category similarity (Brunmair & Richter 2019, g≈0.42 overall).
- DON'T interleave UNRELATED new vocabulary lists (no benefit for heterogeneous word lists, g=-0.39 and non-significant; driven by low item similarity, not "vocab is special").
- DO treat visually confusable kana/kanji (人/入, 大/犬, わ/れ/ね, シ/ツ) as the VISUAL-category case where interleaving wins (paintings g≈0.67), after a blocked introduction of each shape.

### Dual coding & lesson load
- DO pair a new word/kanji with ONE relevant image (word+relevant image beats text alone — Mayer multimedia principle).
- DO pair graphics with spoken narration rather than on-screen text when audio exists (modality principle).
- DON'T add decorative/clip-art visuals or redundant simultaneous text channels (irrelevant visuals and redundant text raise cognitive load — redundancy effect; Sweller/Mayer).
- DO cap new load per lesson (~3–5 grammar / ~15–25 vocab / ~5–10 kanji; ~4–6 exercises; ~2.0k pt-BR chars) — element-interactivity / working-memory limits (cognitive load theory).

### Mnemonics
- DO use keyword/pt-BR mnemonics as an ENCODING ON-RAMP wrapped in ≥2 fed-back retrievals, never as the study event itself (UNRETRIEVED they backfire: 43% vs rote 62% recall at a 2-day delay, ~2× the items lost — [Wang, Thomas, Inzana & Primicerio 1993](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03337348); but at our sparse ~2-retrievals-per-item density the keyword is what makes retrieval beat restudy at all, lifting first-attempt success from ~25% to 43–59%, past the ~50% mark where retrieval starts to pay — [Miyatsu & McDaniel 2019](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-019-00936-2), [Qu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e25212). The advantage flattens to merely additive by ~4 retrievals, and no study runs past 1 week, so treat 8-week durability as untested.)
- DON'T treat a mnemonic as the learning event (picture+sound kana mnemonics helped only some learners on immediate recall and NEITHER group on delayed recall).

### Component-kanji
- DO teach kanji component/radical-first, leading with the SEMANTIC radical, recombining already-known parts (analytic study beats pure rote or pure mnemonics — Flores d'Arcais).
- DO surface the PHONETIC component as a secondary reading aid where it is reliable (learners benefit from both semantic and phonetic functional components).
- DON'T order the kanji syllabus by a single raw frequency list (rankings disagree sharply across corpora — 人 is #1 in Aozora, #7 in Wikipedia; a component-based order retains better for learners outside Japan).
- DON'T lean on radical analysis alone at advanced levels (benefit shrinks with orthography-semantics inconsistency; supplement, don't replace).

### Pitch & pronunciation
- DO teach pronunciation explicitly, targeting SPECIFIC features with BOTH articulatory ("how to produce") and auditory ("how to hear") information (large effect, d≈0.80–0.89 — Lee, Jang & Plonsky 2015; gains strongest on controlled tasks — Saito & Plonsky 2019).
- DO train perception with HVPT-style drills: multiple speakers, varied contexts, forced-choice minimal-pair ID with trial-by-trial feedback (medium-large, durable, partly generalizable perception gains across ~79 studies).
- DO teach pitch as a LISTENING/minimal-pair skill first, with a VISUAL pitch-HEIGHT notation aligned to audio (height notation generalized to untrained words; flat notation and hand-gesture did not — 2024 study). はし (hashí, ponte) vs はし (háshi, palitos).
- DO target intelligibility, not native perfection, and teach pitch SELECTIVELY (high-frequency minimal pairs); state the course teaches Tokyo accent and that Kansai/regional differs (avoid over-generalizing pairs).
- DESIGN CHOICE (heuristic, not a research finding): aim ~80% discrimination before pushing production of a contrast.

### Motivation & affect
- DO design for AUTONOMOUS motivation via autonomy + competence + relatedness: choice, attainable next steps, meaning ("para morar/trabalhar no Japão") (autonomous motivation r≈.23 with achievement — SDT-L2 2025).
- DON'T over-rely on controlled motivators (streaks/points as the core driver): controlled motivation is weakly negative-to-null with achievement (scale-dependent).
- DO message "intelligibility, not perfection" explicitly to defuse pronunciation/pitch perfectionism (foreign-language anxiety suppresses output; acute for solo self-learners).

## B. Japanese-specific sequencing
- DO teach hiragana to mastery FIRST, then katakana on a need-to-read basis, with kanji introduced alongside (kana acquired in parallel in L1 schooling; a strict "all katakana before any kanji" gate is not motivated).
- DO read from kana, not romaji, from the start; keep romaji off by default as a learning SURFACE and measure the reveal rate rather than banning it outright (kana is read via a sublexical phonology route). The warrant is the redundancy effect plus pt-BR grapheme interference (`phonetics_pt_ja.md`): "neko" invites BP final-vowel raising to [ˈneku], "tsu" invites "tu", and the doubled consonant in "kitte" does not cue the っ mora hold. It is NOT Okuyama 2007, a single unreplicated n=61 study that found no LARGE romaji effect, which is a null rather than evidence of harm. "Wean by ~unit 3" is our design choice, not an inherited finding: Marugoto, the Japan Foundation's own reference course, ships romaji throughout A1 against a 60%-kana target. Keep the `romaji` FIELD in corpus data regardless — it is load-bearing for kana instruction, IME input, search and screen readers. (Per `learning_science.md` R62.)
- DO teach the dictionary form and verb-group identification BEFORE the て-form, even though ます-form leads the course (you cannot derive 食べる back from ます reliably: ききます→きく, not *きる).
- DO keep ます-form-first as the default register from lesson 1 (politeness safety, regular negative/past, confidence; positive transfer from você/o senhor → です・ます).
- DO ground は vs が in identifiability / old-vs-new information, not the bare "topic vs subject" gloss (the cost is discourse-processing load; it stays hard into advanced levels).
- DO sequence particles は → が → を → に → で → へ, and treat に vs で (existence/destination vs site-of-action) as the #2 error after は/が; cover を (object), へ (direction), and question-word interaction (誰が…) explicitly.
- DO introduce the native つ/ひとつ・ふたつ series first as the generic fallback (covers 1–10), then 個 as the next generic, then specific counters later (~a dozen high-frequency counters cover most beginner needs; ~30 in active native daily use; hundreds exist).
- DO teach length as SEPARATE drilled skills (long vowel, っ, ん) — perception of one does not generalize to the others; intelligibility is the realistic target.
- DO cover the long-vowel WRITING conventions when teaching the sounds (long /o/ usually おう not おお; long /e/ usually えい; katakana ー; particles は=wa, へ=e, を=o) — high-frequency beginner spelling errors.
- DON'T defer handwriting of kana (and early kanji) — motor production aids RECOGNITION for adult learners (corpus stores KanjiVG stroke order; incumbents drop this — a market gap).

## C. Common mistakes to avoid (BP→JA transfer)
- DON'T let learners hammer/lengthen one mora (BP stress-as-loudness/length breaks both mora timing and pitch; keep moras even — はし pair).
- DON'T let the BP final-vowel reflex raise Japanese finals (BP gato→[ˈgatu]); ねこ is "ne-ko", not "neku"; です keeps its slot (BP /o/→[u], /e/→[i] is automatic — see `phonetics_pt_ja.md`).
- DON'T insert /i/ between consonants (BP "club-i", "stop-i"); Japanese inserts /u/ (and /o/ after t/d: beddo) — cross-default mismatch.
- DON'T round/protrude Japanese /u/ like BP tudo (it is high central-to-back, lips neutral, no forward protrusion).
- DON'T collapse ん into a BP nasal vowel on the preceding vowel (ん is its OWN mora; place assimilates to the next sound).
- DON'T under-hold っ (BP has no length-contrastive consonants; learners systematically shorten it — hold the full silent beat).
- DON'T over-supply pronouns/subjects, especially あなた (Japanese is pro-drop; explicit pronouns sound unnatural where Japanese drops them).
- DON'T front-load the verb (Japanese is rigidly verb-final SOV; "hold the verb to the end").
- DON'T map BP "ter" (existential) onto いる/ある without the animate/inanimate split, and DON'T over-supply the copula (です/だ register + drop) — concrete BP→JA errors.
- DON'T over-trust gairaigo: Portuguese-origin loans are a hook (pan→パン, botão→ボタン, tabaco→タバコ, copo→コップ, capa→カッパ) but meanings drift and pronunciation is full-mora (コップ ≠ copo); wasei-eigo differs (マンション = apartamento, サラリーマン); the learner's English-loan intuition is partial (ビール beer vs ビル building).
- DON'T let mnemonics or romaji become the durable representation (both are on-ramps; wrap mnemonics in retrieval, wean romaji).

## D. Lesson auditor checklist (apply per lesson)

> Each rule is a pass/fail flag for ONE lesson; the Phase-2 auditor and every future authoring prompt use it.

1. Has ≥1 active-RETRIEVAL exercise AND ≥1 PRODUCTION/sentence-construction exercise (not all re-recognition).
2. Every production/recall exercise has an answer key + corrective feedback/explanation (feedback rescues failed retrieval).
3. The lesson's first encounter of each new item is supported enough to make the first GRADED retrieval likely successful (worked example / model before the test). A `pretest` exercise — a deliberate pre-instruction guess — is exempt and does not count as that first retrieval. (Amended per `learning_science.md` R18: a pre-instruction guess precedes support by construction, so without the exemption every unit using a pretest fails this check.)
4. No passive-rereading-only "exercise" stands in for retrieval.
5. New-load cap respected: ~3–5 new grammar / ~15–25 new vocab / ~5–10 new kanji; ~4–6 exercises; content prose ~2.0k pt-BR chars (light lesson).
6. i+1: introduces ≤1 item beyond the cumulative known set; every example sentence is ~100% known-tokens except that single i+1 item.
7. Dependency order holds (no て-form before dictionary-form group ID; no relative clause before plain past; no comparative before adjectives).
8. Confusable sets present in the lesson are introduced BLOCKED, then flagged for interleaved review (は/が, に/で, transitive/intransitive, look-alike kana/kanji, length minimal pairs); unrelated vocab is NOT interleaved.
9. Kanji taught component/SEMANTIC-radical first with a pt-BR (not translated-from-English) mnemonic; mnemonic is wrapped in a later retrieval, not presented as the learning event. EXEMPT: a kanji whose gloss names no picturable referent (88 of 630 N5–N3 characters, e.g. 意 感 決 変 対 関 続) needs no keyword mnemonic — but component decomposition still applies to it, since decomposition is semantic analysis of an orthographic form, a different mechanism from the keyword method. (Amended per `learning_science.md` R4: without this line, D.9 and the imageability requirement in R3 are jointly unsatisfiable.)
10. Vocab/kanji carry ONE relevant image hook (no decorative clip-art); no redundant simultaneous romaji+kana+kanji where it adds load (redundancy effect).
11. Any pronunciation/phonetics claim is correct per `phonetics_pt_ja.md` (no "lips relaxed" /u/, no bare "tchi" for ち, no fixed long-vowel ratio, no "guttural coda-r everywhere", no つ="tu").
12. Where the lesson's content triggers one, the correct Vantagem PT or Armadilha PT callout is present, using the exact corrective phrasing pattern from the phonetics doc.
13. Callouts use the block TYPE only (`<note type="l1-advantage|l1-pitfall|tip|warning|culture|example">`); no emoji anywhere in learner text.
14. pt-BR with correct diacritics throughout (no accent-stripped prose); never pt-PT; no em dash in authored learner prose; no "AI tells" (run humanizer).
15. Romaji is not a learning SURFACE: no Latin-script reading in an exercise stem, in an answer option, or as the primary form of a vocab/phrase card. Past ~unit 3 it is reveal-on-demand support only, and the reveal rate is logged rather than assumed (per `learning_science.md` R62). The `romaji` corpus FIELD is unaffected.
16. Kana/letters treated as DISCRETE units (one beat per mora); mora/length/ん/っ taught as the clap model, not as BP nasal vowels or stress.
17. Pitch (if present) is taught perception-first with visual pitch-HEIGHT notation + a minimal pair; framed as intelligibility (Tokyo accent), not native perfection.
18. Grammar terms used are defined on first use and match the curriculum glossary; は/が grounded in old-vs-new info, not bare "topic vs subject".
19. Example sentences prefer REAL (ai_generated=0, Tatoeba/JEC) over AI; sentences referenced by ID only (no embedded text/dissection); generated jp drops 。.
20. Spacing hook present: new items are tagged/chunked so a later SRS can schedule them (introduce → expanding-lag review); prior items spiral in.
21. Translations are natural pt-BR in `translation` (literal mirror only in `translation_literal`); no "Quanto a mim" crutch in the natural field.
22. No meta/dev text in any learner field; identifiers (slug/topic/ref) stay ASCII; word-separating spaces at tag boundaries intact.
23. BP→JA transfer traps relevant to this lesson are addressed (verb-final/hold-the-verb, pro-drop/no あなた over-supply, ter→いる/ある animacy, copula drop, /i/-vs-/u/ epenthesis, final-vowel raising) where the content surfaces them.
24. Counters (if taught) lead with the native つ/ひとつ・ふたつ fallback (covers 1–10), then 個, then specific counters; long-vowel WRITING conventions (おう/えい/ー, は=wa/へ=e/を=o) taught alongside the sounds.
